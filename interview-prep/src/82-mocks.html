Mocks & self-grading

Four graded mocks are scheduled — days 14, 21, and two on 27. A mock with a real person is better, but a solo mock done seriously is worth far more than another chapter of reading, and you can run one tonight.

How to run one alone

  1. Pick a prompt from the bank below without reading ahead. Set a timer for 45 minutes.
  2. Record yourself — audio is enough, and it is the entire trick. You will hear the filler, the tangents, and the ninety-second silences that you cannot feel from the inside.
  3. Work on paper or a blank whiteboard tool. No searching, no notes, no tabs.
  4. Talk the whole time, to an imagined interviewer. If it feels absurd, good — that discomfort is the thing you're desensitizing.
  5. When the timer ends, stop mid-sentence. Then grade yourself on the rubric before listening back.
  6. Listen at 1.5×. Write down every moment you'd want to redo. Those go on the gap list for day 25.

Reading the score: mostly 3s is a hire. Any 1 is a specific, fixable thing — the most common by far are estimation (fix: §10, out loud, daily) and depth (fix: pick your dive earlier and go two more levels than feels necessary). Track the same six numbers across all four mocks; you are looking for the trend, not the absolute.

The gap list

Keep one file for the whole month. Every mock, every blank-page exercise, every problem you couldn't finish adds one line: what you missed, and the one sentence you'd say instead. By day 25 it will be twenty or thirty lines — and that file, not this page, is your actual study material for the last week. Prep works when it converges on your specific weaknesses instead of re-reading what you already know.
