ML design framework

An ML system design round is a system design round with two extra loops bolted on: a training loop that produces models from data, and a feedback loop where your predictions become tomorrow's training data. Candidates who only design the inference path get read as "knows models, hasn't shipped one".

The eight steps

#StepWhat to say
1Frame itTurn the product ask into an ML task: what is the input, what is the output, what is one training example? "Recommend videos" becomes "given (user, candidate video, context), predict P(watch > 30 s)".
2MetricsThree tiers: business (retention, revenue), online (CTR, watch time — what the A/B test measures), offline (AUC, NDCG@10 — what you optimize daily). Plus explicit guardrails you must not regress.
3Data & labelsWhere does the label come from and how long until you have it? Implicit feedback is free and biased; explicit is clean and scarce. Name the delay: fraud labels arrive with a 30-day chargeback lag.
4FeaturesGroup them: user, item, user×item interaction, context (time, device, location). Say which are precomputed batch, which are real-time, and which are too expensive to compute per request.
5ModelAlways start with a baseline (popularity, logistic regression, gradient-boosted trees) and justify complexity against it. Jumping straight to a transformer without a baseline is a judgment flag.
6TrainingCadence, data volume, negative sampling, class imbalance, point-in-time correctness. How do you retrain — and how do you roll back?
7ServingLatency budget, batch vs real-time, model size vs QPS, caching, and the fallback when the model service is unavailable.
8MonitorPrediction distribution, feature drift, label delay, online/offline metric divergence, and the feedback-loop pathologies below.

The four failures that separate levels

Training / serving skew

Features computed one way in a Spark job and another way in the serving path. The model sees a different world in production than in training, and the metric silently degrades. Fix: a feature store with one definition compiled to both paths, plus a continuous check comparing logged serving features against recomputed training features.

Label leakage

A feature that encodes the future — "number of items in cart at checkout" predicting purchase, or a feature aggregated over the whole dataset including post-event rows. Fix: point-in-time correct joins — every feature value is as of the prediction timestamp, never later. This is the single hardest thing about feature infrastructure.

Feedback loops

You only get labels for what you showed. The model recommends what it already believes, gets confirming clicks, and narrows forever. Fix: log the propensity of each impression, use inverse-propensity weighting, and reserve a small random-exposure slice as unbiased evaluation data.

Position & presentation bias

The top slot gets clicked because it is the top slot. Train naively on clicks and you learn "position 1 is good". Fix: include position as a feature during training and set it to a constant at inference, or model click = P(examined | position) × P(relevant).

Offline is a filter; online is the truth

Deployment is a staircase, and being able to name it is a strong shipping signal: offline eval on a held-out time-based split (never random — that leaks the future) → shadow mode, where the new model scores real traffic and its outputs are logged but not shown → interleaving for ranking, which needs a fraction of the traffic of an A/B test → canary at 1% → A/B test sized by a power calculation → ramp, with automatic rollback wired to the guardrail metrics.

The A/B numbers to have ready

Sample size per arm ≈ 16 σ² / Δ² for detecting an absolute lift Δ at 80% power and 95% significance. For a 2% baseline CTR and a 5% relative lift you need roughly a few hundred thousand users per arm — run it for at least one full week to absorb day-of-week effects. Randomize by user, not by request, or the same person sees both variants. Watch for network effects (social features spill between arms — switch to cluster randomization) and novelty effects (an early lift that decays by week two).
